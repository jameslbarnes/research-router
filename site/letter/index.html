Bargaining For Our Minds

An open letter from scientists to the frontier labs

To the frontier labs,

Our published research has helped build your models. As we do more research with your tools, our conversations and agent sessions capture new ideas and the judgement behind them. This growing record of scientific thinking can help you build more capable AI. We are also learning how these systems could threaten our survival.

We want these systems to advance science and improve people’s lives. We have a responsibility to help shape their development, and we need a voice in those decisions now.

We commit to granting permission to train on our future research conversations and agent sessions only through a collective bargaining agreement. Until then, we will keep optional training permissions disabled wherever we control them. We ask you to guarantee that these records will be used for training only with our permission and on the terms of that agreement.

We invite you to negotiate terms that fairly compensate us for our contributions and give us a continuing say in how these systems are developed and used.

About the agreement

The letter sets out a shared position. The questions below explain what we would bring to negotiations and what the agreement would need to settle.

Our contribution Why together Scope Value Our say Signing

Our contribution

What do we contribute when we work with AI?

A scientist may work through an unpublished idea with an AI tool, then correct an answer or explain why an approach will fail. That exchange records original research and judgement developed over years of work.

As models become more capable, we can bring more demanding work into these exchanges. Permission to train on this record is something we can negotiate together. We want these contributions to improve AI and to share in the value they create.

Why together

Why bargain as a group?

Where we control training permissions, each of us can decide whether to enable them. A shared commitment lets us put common terms to the labs and choose representatives to negotiate them.

The basis for negotiation is permission to train on future research conversations and agent sessions. Together, we can offer access to records of scientific judgement on agreed terms. Scientists would retain the choice of which records to contribute.

Scope

What would the agreement cover?

The agreement would cover permission to train on records of future research conversations and agent sessions. These records can include unpublished work we bring into a session, as well as ideas we develop while using the tools.

Our commitment applies where we control that permission. Publication and collaboration remain governed by existing agreements and institutional obligations. Any future contribution would also depend on the rights and permissions held by the people involved.

The agreement would specify which records it covers and which uses it permits. It would also need a way to check compliance and address breaches.

Sharing value

How would our contributions be valued?

That is one of the things the agreement must establish. The letter asks for an appraisal method that scientists and labs can both examine and challenge.

The assessment should explain what is being valued and the evidence behind it. It should account for how contributions become useful together and inform fair compensation.

A continuing say

What would a seat at the table involve?

Scientists’ representatives would take part in decisions about how the labs develop and use the systems our contributions help build. Regular reviews would give them a way to examine what is happening and raise concerns that the labs must address.

The agreement would need to define that role. Scientists should be able to choose their representatives and hold them accountable.

Taking part

What does signing mean?

Signing means committing to grant permission to train on future research conversations and agent sessions only through a collective bargaining agreement. Until then, keep optional training permissions disabled wherever you control them.

Review the tools you use, including any separate permissions for feedback or agent environments. If your institution controls the settings, ask its administrator to review them. The guide explains each provider's training terms and exceptions.
